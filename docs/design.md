# Abundant Waters

*A Boundary Waters canoe adventure game*

Design notes — v2, Sept 2026
Source material: "BWCA Sawbill Family Trip Itinerary — Carlson Family, August 2026"

## Tone & style

Sneaky Sasquatch style: open world, low stress, goofy physics, no fail states that punish. You learn by doing, not by tutorial. Chase memories, not miles.

## Premise

You're a kid on a family canoe trip — a family of six. Each lake is a zone. The trip doc's route (Sawbill → Smoke → Burnt) becomes the stage progression.

## Stage 1 — Sawbill: Paddling

- Learn the J-stroke via a "wobble meter." Sloppy strokes zigzag you into the reeds.
- Sweep stroke, draw stroke, and the bow/stern partnership unlock as you go.
- Wind and waves scale with how far from shore you drift.

## Stage 2 — Portages

- Mini-game about load balancing: pack weight, canoe carry, and not clocking your sibling with the bow.
- Multiple trips vs. one heavy haul — a real tradeoff.
- Muddy landings, rocks, and the classic portage-trail slip.

## Stage 3 — Map & navigation

- No quest markers. You read the actual shoreline against a paper map.
- Identify bays, narrows, and portage landings by their shape.
- Getting lost is recoverable and kind of funny.

## Stage 4 — Camp (home base)

Family jobs, each a small physics toy:

- Tarp line and knots
- Firewood gathering and fire building
- Water filtering
- **Bear bag:** throw a rope over a branch and hoist. Hang it lazy and a bear sorts you out at 2 a.m.

Animal encounters: bear, loons, moose, chipmunks that raid a careless pack.

## Stage 5 — Fishing (the food economy)

- Walleye at dusk, smallmouth off the rocks, northern pike in the weeds.
- Map skill pays off: read shoreline for drop-offs instead of following a marker.
- Catch feeds the family; a bad fishing day means oatmeal again.

## Stage 6 — Underwater: snorkel & dive

- Swim off camp rocks and the canoe with a mask and snorkel.
- Clear water lets you spot fish before you cast — scouting feeds the fishing economy.
- Discoveries: sunken logs, lost paddles and lures, old voyageur-era artifacts, crayfish, freshwater sponges.
- Teaching moments: buddy system, cold-water limits, staying near shore, knowing when to get out.
- Found items become collectibles and can be logged in the Passport.

## Stage 7 — Storm day (weather)

- Weather builds visibly: wind shifts, clouds stack, the lake goes dark.
- Teaching moment: get off the water early, never paddle in lightning, secure the canoe high on shore.
- Race to tarp up camp, stash gear, and batten down before it hits.
- Then a forced slow moment: the family waits it out under the tarp — cards, snacks, and story time.
- Story time unlocks family lore and memories; the storm passes into a rainbow or a glass-calm evening.

## Stage 8 — Night sky

- End-of-day reward. Lie back and connect constellations by hand.
- Unlock lore cards for each one.
- Northern lights appear rarely, so they feel earned.

## Progression: Adventure Passport

Straight from the family field guide — stamps instead of a score.

- Each family member has their own Passport.
- Stamps for skills and moments: first clean J-stroke, one-trip portage, perfect bear hang, first walleye, first dive discovery, weathering a storm, spotting the northern lights.
- Wildlife spotted (loon, moose, bear, eagle, beaver) fills a sightings page.
- Fish Log and Journal pages fill in as you play, mirroring the real trip journals.
- A completed Passport unlocks the next year's trip — a new entry point and new lakes.
